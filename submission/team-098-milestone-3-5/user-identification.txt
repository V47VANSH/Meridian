Team 98 | IITM BS Degree, Software Engineering | May 2026
User Identification


PROBLEM CONTEXT
===============

Apartment societies coordinate maintenance collection, complaints, shared
facilities, visitors and emergencies through spreadsheets, WhatsApp groups,
phone calls and paper registers. Our user research surfaced five recurring pain
points, each traceable to the same root cause - nothing is recorded anywhere
both sides can see:

  1. No visibility into what is owed, or whether a payment was received.
  2. Complaints raised verbally, leaving no trace and no accountability.
  3. Shared facilities double-booked because the calendar lives in one head.
  4. Visitors admitted on the strength of a phone call to the resident.
  5. Owners living elsewhere with no way to see any of the above.

The platform is built as multi-tenant SaaS: one deployment serves many
societies, each isolated by tenant_id. Users therefore exist within a society,
except for the platform operator who sits above all of them.


PRIMARY USERS
=============
The people who use the system most often, and whose problems it exists to
solve. Every core workflow begins or ends with one of them.

1. RESIDENT / FLAT OWNER  (role: RESIDENT_OWNER)
   Lives in the society and holds or rents a flat.
   Pain points addressed:
     - Cannot see what maintenance is due until told.
     - Complaints disappear once spoken aloud.
     - No reliable way to reserve a shared facility.
     - Must telephone the gate to admit each visitor.
   Capabilities:
     - View and pay maintenance invoices; see payment history.
     - Raise complaints and follow their status through to resolution.
     - View facility availability, book a slot, cancel a booking.
     - Issue and revoke single-use digital guest passes.
     - Raise an emergency SOS alert.
     - Read society announcements and personal notifications.

2. SOCIETY OFFICIAL  (role: SOCIETY_OFFICIAL)
   Manages the society's day-to-day operations; typically a committee member.
   Pain points addressed:
     - Chasing dues without a record of who has paid.
     - No queue or priority order for incoming complaints.
     - Facility clashes escalating into disputes.
     - No single place holding society records.
   Capabilities:
     - Raise invoices, record offline payments, send reminders, view
       collection summaries.
     - Triage complaints: reprioritise, assign to staff, advance status.
     - Publish and black out facility slots; view the society-wide schedule.
     - Maintain the resident directory; create users and link them to flats.
     - Post announcements to a chosen audience.
     - Review the audit log of state-changing actions.


SECONDARY USERS
===============
Depend on the system but interact with it less frequently, or in a narrower
way, than primary users.

3. NON-RESIDENT FLAT OWNER  (role: NON_RESIDENT_OWNER)
   Owns a flat but lives elsewhere; often the flat is tenanted.
   Pain points addressed:
     - No remote visibility of dues, complaints or society decisions.
     - Cannot help a tenant admit a visitor while away.
   Capabilities:
     - Track dues and complaint status for the owned flat remotely.
     - Issue guest passes for the flat without being present.
     - Read announcements and notifications.

4. VISITOR / GUEST  (no account)
   A guest, delivery agent, cab driver or service visitor attending a flat.
   Deliberately NOT a registered user. A visitor holds a signed, time-boxed,
   single-use pass token issued by a resident. This keeps the authentication
   model simple - no visitor registration or login - while still giving the
   guard something cryptographically verifiable to check at the gate.
   Capabilities:
     - Present a QR-encoded pass at the gate for verification.


TERTIARY USERS
==============
Society staff who act on work the system generates. They consume tasks and
alerts rather than initiating workflows.

5. SECURITY GUARD  (role: SECURITY_GUARD)
   Staffs the gate and responds to emergencies.
   Pain points addressed:
     - No way to confirm an unexpected visitor is genuinely expected.
     - No record of who entered and when.
     - Emergency calls arriving by shouting or telephone.
   Capabilities:
     - Verify a guest pass; the pass is consumed on first successful use, so a
       photographed QR cannot be replayed.
     - Receive, acknowledge and resolve SOS alerts.

6. MAINTENANCE STAFF  (role: MAINTENANCE_STAFF)
   Carries out repairs assigned by an official.
   Capabilities:
     - View complaints assigned to them, ordered by urgency.
     - Advance complaint status as work progresses, including a one-tap
       resolve for issues fixed on the spot.

7. CLEANING STAFF  (role: CLEANING_STAFF)
   Handles cleaning and hygiene tasks raised as complaints.
   Capabilities:
     - View and progress assigned cleaning complaints.

8. SERVICE PROVIDER  (role: SERVICE_PROVIDER)
   External contractor engaged for specialist work.
   Capabilities:
     - Receive and fulfil assigned service requests.


PLATFORM USER
=============
Sits above every society rather than inside one.

9. PLATFORM ADMINISTRATOR  (role: SUPER_ADMIN)
   Operates the platform itself; not a member of any society.
   Capabilities:
     - Onboard and manage societies (tenants).
     - Has no tenant_id, and therefore no access to any society's operational
       data through the tenant-scoped endpoints.

ACCESS CONTROL SUMMARY
======================

Every request's society scope is taken from the authenticated token's claim,
never from a request body or query parameter, so a user cannot ask for another
society's data. Role permissions are enforced as route dependencies.

A cross-society read returns 404 rather than 403: a 403 would confirm the
record exists, which itself leaks that another society holds that identifier.
